# Analysis Notes

## Core Positional Findings

### Quarterbacks (QB)
Quarterbacks were approximately 65% White in the modern NFL dataset, despite the league overall being majority Black. This suggests that the quarterback position continues to retain disproportionate White representation relative to many other positions.

### Running Backs (RB)
Running backs were approximately 80.41% Black, making RB one of the most racially concentrated positions in the dataset. This aligns with scholarship discussing racial stacking and the historical association of Black athletes with physically intensive labor roles.

### Wide Receivers (WR)
Wide receivers were approximately 72.15% Black. This may reflect longstanding stereotypes associating Black athletes with speed and explosiveness rather than leadership or strategic intelligence.

### Defensive Backs (DB)
Defensive backs were approximately 70.56% Black. DB positions involve high-speed collisions and reaction-based athleticism, historically associated with racialized assumptions about instinctive rather than intellectual play.

### Offensive Line (OL)
Offensive line positions were approximately 48.8% White, making OL one of the whitest major position groups in the dataset. OL positions are often associated with strategic protection schemes and leadership communication responsibilities.

### Tight Ends (TE)
Tight ends were approximately 65.62% White. TE serves as a hybrid position combining blocking intelligence and route responsibilities, potentially reflecting historical biases surrounding football intelligence and versatility.

## Initial Interpretation

The modern NFL dataset suggests that positional demographics remain highly uneven across racial lines. Leadership-oriented or strategically coded positions such as quarterback and tight end remain disproportionately White, while many physically hazardous and collision-heavy positions remain disproportionately Black.

These patterns support scholarship arguing that historical racial stacking within football development systems continues to shape contemporary positional outcomes.
